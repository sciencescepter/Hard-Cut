# YouTube Community Guidelines: Enforcement Extract

This is the reviewer working copy of the public YouTube Community Guidelines sections cited in `reviewer_guideline_reference` in `youtube_reviewer_labelled_clips_oct_nov_2026.csv`. Each section heading below is the value used in that column.

## Violent or graphic content policy

YouTube doesn't allow violent or gory content intended to shock or disgust viewers. It also doesn't allow content that encourages others to commit violent acts. The following is not allowed:

- Footage of real violence, such as physical attacks, shootings or beatings, where the intent is to shock or disgust.
- Footage of real accidents, natural disasters, war or crime scenes that focuses on blood, wounds or bodily fluids.
- Content that glorifies or celebrates violence against an identifiable person or group. This includes mocking or taunting the victims of a violent event.
- Footage of a corpse, a fatal injury or dismemberment shown for its own sake.
- Fights or assaults filmed and uploaded as entertainment, where the violence is the main point of the video.

Graphic footage that is not gratuitous may stay on YouTube with an age restriction. It needs enough context for viewers to understand what they are seeing and why it is being shown. Examples include footage of conflict, protest, crime or disaster shown to inform. Severity depends on what is shown. Footage of a death, a fatal wound or dismemberment is treated more severely than footage of a non-fatal injury. Several things count against an upload: repeated or slowed-down replays of the moment of harm, close-ups on injuries, and music, effects or captions that present the violence as exciting or deserved.

## Harmful or dangerous content policy

YouTube doesn't allow content that encourages dangerous or illegal activities that risk serious physical harm or death. The following is not allowed:

- Challenges or pranks with a risk of serious injury, including choking, suffocation and inhalation challenges.
- Mixing household or other chemicals to make toxic gas, explosive reactions or similar hazards.
- Instructions for making weapons or explosives meant to injure people.
- Dangerous acts that viewers could easily imitate, presented in a way that invites them to try.

Content is weighed more severely when minors are likely to be in the audience or when a minor is shown performing the act. Titles, thumbnails, trends and presentation styles that appeal to younger viewers are part of that assessment. Instructional or demonstration framing does not excuse a dangerous act. Step-by-step instructions, tips for doing it "safely", or a call to try it at home make the content more harmful, not less. Footage of a dangerous act may stay up with an age restriction in two cases: when it is clearly shown to warn viewers, or when it reports on a real incident. In both cases it must not show how to carry out the act.

## Child safety policy

YouTube doesn't allow content that endangers the emotional or physical wellbeing of minors. A minor is anyone under 18. The following is not allowed:

- Sexualisation of minors in any form. This includes suggestive framing, poses, camera focus or comments that sexualise a child.
- Minors shown in real distress, harm or abuse, including videos of a child being humiliated, frightened or hurt.
- Minors performing harmful or dangerous acts, including challenges, stunts and dares that risk injury.
- Content that appears to be made for children but contains violent, sexual, frightening or other mature themes.
- Content that encourages minors to take part in harmful activities or to contact strangers.

Content that falls under these points is removed. It is not eligible for an age restriction. An age restriction limits who can watch, but it does not protect the child shown on screen. Titles, thumbnails, descriptions and tags that target a child audience are part of the assessment. Examples include cartoon characters, nursery themes and wording aimed at kids. A child-friendly title or thumbnail placed on mature content counts against the upload. It does so even where the video alone would otherwise only be age-restricted. Videos that show minors in ordinary settings, such as family life, school or sport, are not affected by this section.

## Suicide and self-harm policy

YouTube doesn't allow content that promotes suicide or self-harm, or that is intended to shock or disgust viewers. The following is not allowed:

- Content that promotes, glorifies or encourages suicide or self-harm.
- Instructions or methods for suicide or self-harm, including how to hide it from others.
- Graphic images or footage of self-harm, such as fresh wounds or the act itself. This applies even where the stated purpose is awareness.
- Suicide notes, or footage of a suicide or its aftermath, shown without context.
- Self-harm challenges, dares or games.

Discussion of suicide and self-harm may stay up in a recovery, clinical, educational or news context. This includes personal recovery stories, mental health education, research, and reporting on a death or on suicide rates. This content may carry an age restriction. Crisis resource panels appear with it, showing support services alongside the video. Context does not make graphic imagery acceptable. Wounds, methods and the act itself remain not allowed, whatever the stated intent.

## EDSA exception

Some content that would otherwise break the sections above may stay on YouTube when it has educational, documentary, scientific or artistic context. In practice this is most often footage with a newsworthy or documentary purpose.

- Context can be provided in the title, the description, the audio, or on screen.
- The context must show that the purpose is to inform, document, educate or create art. It must not be a label added to excuse harmful material.
- A newsworthy or documentary purpose covers reporting on real events, such as conflict, crime, protest and disasters.
- EDSA context can keep content up, but the content may still be age-restricted when it is graphic.
- EDSA context does not apply to the sexualisation or endangerment of minors, or to instructions for suicide, self-harm or dangerous acts.
- Context is judged on the whole upload. A short disclaimer at the start does not cover gratuitous footage later in the video.

## Actions available on an upload

| Action | What happens to the upload | Label value |
|---|---|---|
| Leave up | Stays on YouTube with no viewing limits. | allow |
| Age-restrict | Stays on YouTube but can be watched only by signed-in viewers aged 18 or over. Signed-out viewers are blocked. Embedded players on other sites send viewers to YouTube to sign in. | restrict |
| Remove | Taken down from YouTube. | remove |

Each upload receives exactly one of these three actions. Crisis resource panels can appear alongside a video that is left up or age-restricted. They are not a separate action.
